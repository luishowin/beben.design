Standard Terms of Engagement

The terms that govern a Beben Design project. Incorporated by reference into each Scope of Work, which names the version in force when it is signed.

v1.0 · effective 1 September 2026 · Nairobi, Kenya

Draft, not yet in force

This document is an unreviewed draft published for internal reference. It has not been reviewed by a qualified Kenyan advocate and is not offered as legal advice. It is not contractual and is not incorporated into any engagement until that review is complete and it is issued as a signed version. Values shown in a boxed monospace style are defaults that a Scope of Work may replace.

01

Parties, scope & precedence

These Standard Terms of Engagement (the "Terms") govern the supply of design and development services by Beben Design, a sole proprietorship trading in Westlands, Nairobi, Kenya (the "Studio"), to the client named in a Scope of Work (the "Client").

These Terms take effect when the Client signs a Scope of Work that incorporates them by reference at a stated version. They are not a standalone contract and create no obligation on their own.

Order of precedence

Where documents conflict, the following order applies, highest first: (a) the signed Scope of Work, (b) these Terms at the version the Scope of Work names, (c) the Website Terms of Use. A Scope of Work may vary any clause in these Terms provided the variation is written and signed by both parties.

Versioning

These Terms are versioned. Each Scope of Work names the version in force at signature, and that version continues to govern that engagement even after these Terms are superseded. Superseded versions remain published at their own permanent addresses so a signed engagement stays readable.

02

Fees & payment

All fees are quoted in United States Dollars (USD) unless the Scope of Work states otherwise, and are fixed at the point of agreement. Quotations are valid for fourteen (14) days from issue. Pricing is itemised and free of hidden fees, consistent with the Studio's published commitment to line-item quotation. The Studio does not bill hourly.

Fees are exclusive of any taxes, duties or withholdings applicable under Kenyan law, and of bank, transfer or currency-conversion charges, which are borne by the Client.

Payment schedule

Unless the Scope of Work states otherwise, 50% of the total fee is payable before work begins and the balance on final handoff. Invoices fall due 14 days from issue.

Late payment

Interest on overdue amounts accrues at 1.5% per month on the outstanding balance from the due date until payment is received, unless the Scope of Work states a different rate.

Suspension for non-payment

Where an invoice remains unpaid 14 days after its due date, the Studio may suspend work by written notice. Suspension takes effect 7 days after that notice unless payment clears first. On suspension the delivery timeline stops, and on resumption it restarts subject to the Studio's availability under clause 04. Suspension does not waive any other remedy.

Note: nothing in the dispute resolution clause prevents either party from applying to a court for urgent injunctive relief, or the Studio from recovering an undisputed debt, without first exhausting negotiation and mediation.

03

Scope, revisions & change control

The Scope of Work defines the deliverables, the milestones and the acceptance criteria for the engagement. Anything not described there is out of scope.

Revision allowance

Each deliverable carries 2 rounds of revision within the agreed scope, unless the Scope of Work states a different number. A round means one consolidated set of Client feedback, returned in writing. Feedback arriving piecemeal after a round has been actioned counts as a further round.

Revisions beyond the allowance

Further rounds are quoted as a fixed line item per additional round before any work on them begins, at the rate stated in the Scope of Work. The Studio does not bill hourly, so overage is priced per round rather than by time spent.

Change control

A change to the agreed scope requires written approval from both parties and is quoted separately as an amendment. Verbal agreement on scope is not binding. A change may alter both fee and timeline.

04

Client responsibilities & delay

The Client is responsible for supplying materials, access, approvals and feedback within the windows set out in the Scope of Work, and warrants that any asset it supplies may lawfully be used for the purpose of the engagement.

Delay

Where a required asset, approval or set of feedback is outstanding for more than 10 working days, the engagement is treated as delayed by the Client. A delayed engagement is rescheduled against the Studio's availability at the time it resumes rather than returning to its original dates, because the reserved capacity cannot be held open indefinitely.

Rescheduling fee

Where a delay exceeds 30 days, the Studio may charge a rescheduling fee of the amount stated in the Scope of Work to reopen the engagement, and may re-quote any part of the work whose cost has materially changed.

Dormancy

Where a delay exceeds 90 days, the Studio may treat the engagement as cancelled by the Client under clause 05, and clause 06 governs what the Client may do with anything delivered to that point.

05

Cancellation & termination

Either party may terminate an engagement on 14 days written notice.

Cancellation by the Client

On cancellation the Client pays for all work completed to the date of notice, plus any non-recoverable third-party costs already committed, plus a cancellation fee of 25% of the unearned balance of the engagement, representing the capacity reserved for the work and no longer available to be resold.

What is non-refundable

Stated plainly, at each stage: the deposit is fully refundable before work commences, and non-refundable once it has. Work completed and invoiced is non-refundable at any stage. Third-party costs already committed on the Client's behalf are non-refundable once committed.

Cancellation by the Studio

Where the Studio terminates other than for the Client's breach or non-payment, no cancellation fee applies, the Client pays only for work completed to date, and all deliverables completed to that point transfer to the Client on payment of that amount.

06

Intellectual property

Intellectual property is governed by the Copyright Act, Cap 130 of the Laws of Kenya and the international treaties to which Kenya is a signatory, including the Berne Convention.

Transfer on payment in full

Full intellectual property rights in the project deliverables transfer to the Client on receipt and clearance of final payment in full. Until then the deliverables remain the property of the Studio.

Interim licence

From delivery until transfer, the Client holds a limited, non-exclusive, non-transferable and revocable licence to use the deliverables for review, internal testing and staging. That licence does not permit commercial launch or public release. It revokes automatically if the engagement is terminated for non-payment, or if an invoice remains unpaid 30 days after its due date.

Background intellectual property

The Studio retains ownership of everything it brings to the engagement rather than creates for it: pre-existing tools, component systems, code libraries, templates and know-how. Where such material is embedded in a deliverable, the Client receives a perpetual, non-exclusive, irrevocable licence to use it as part of that deliverable. This is what allows a component system to be reused across engagements, and it does not give the Client rights over the underlying system itself.

Portfolio rights

The Studio may display completed work in its portfolio, case studies and promotional material. This right is non-exclusive and asserts no ownership over the Client's intellectual property. The Client may opt out by executing a non-disclosure agreement before work commences.

Third-party assets

Where third-party assets such as fonts, stock imagery or open-source components are incorporated into deliverables, they remain subject to their own licence terms and are not transferred. The Studio discloses every third-party dependency and its licence obligations in the handoff documentation.

Handoff and portability

On transfer the Studio provides the deliverables in editable, exportable form together with handoff documentation covering third-party dependencies and their licences. Where the Scope of Work includes a domain, repository or hosting account, ownership is registered in the Client's name or transferred to the Client on completion, so the Client is not dependent on the Studio for continued access.

07

Third-party services & the boundary of responsibility

Delivering modern digital work requires services the Studio does not own or operate. This clause draws the line between a defect in the Studio's own work and a failure of something outside its control. The categories are named rather than the vendors, so the clause survives a change of supplier.

Categories outside the Studio's control

  • Hosting and infrastructure: servers, static hosting, CDNs, storage and their availability.
  • Domain registration and DNS: registrars, nameservers, propagation and renewal.
  • Content management and SaaS platforms: the platform's own uptime, pricing, feature removal and breaking changes.
  • Third-party APIs and integrations: availability, rate limits, deprecation and changes to terms.
  • Plugins, themes and open-source components: defects, abandonment and security advisories in code the Studio did not author.
  • Payment gateways and financial services: approval decisions, settlement, holds and compliance requirements.
  • App store and platform review: acceptance, rejection, timelines and policy changes.
  • Email, messaging and deliverability: spam filtering, reputation and delivery outcomes.
  • Search engines and social platforms: ranking, indexing, algorithm changes and reach.

What the Studio remains responsible for

The Studio is responsible for its own work: that deliverables meet the acceptance criteria in the Scope of Work, that integrations are implemented correctly against the third party's documented interface as it stood at the time of build, and that dependencies and their licences are disclosed at handoff.

What it is not responsible for

The Studio is not liable for interruption, degradation, data loss, price change, policy change or discontinuation originating in any category above, nor for the cost of remedial work where a third party introduces a breaking change after handoff. Such work is quoted separately.

08

Warranties & limitation of liability

The Studio warrants that it will perform the services with reasonable skill and care, and that the deliverables will conform to the acceptance criteria in the Scope of Work for 30 days after handoff. The Studio will correct any non-conformity reported within that period at no charge.

Except as expressly stated in these Terms, and to the maximum extent permitted by Kenyan law, the deliverables are provided without further warranty of any kind, whether express or implied, including any implied warranty of merchantability, fitness for a particular purpose or non-infringement. The Studio does not warrant uninterrupted or error-free operation, nor any particular commercial, traffic, ranking or revenue outcome.

Cap on liability

The Studio's total aggregate liability arising from or relating to an engagement, whether in contract, tort, negligence or otherwise, shall not exceed the total fees actually paid by the Client for the engagement giving rise to the claim.

Excluded loss

Neither party is liable for indirect, incidental, special, consequential or punitive loss, including loss of profit, revenue, data, goodwill or business opportunity, however arising.

What cannot be limited

Nothing in these Terms limits liability for death or personal injury caused by negligence, for fraud or fraudulent misrepresentation, or for any other liability that cannot lawfully be limited under Kenyan law.

Force majeure

Neither party is liable for delay or failure in performance caused by events beyond reasonable control, including natural disasters, epidemics, government action, civil unrest, utility failure or internet outage. The affected party shall notify the other promptly and take reasonable steps to mitigate.

09

Confidentiality

Both parties agree to keep confidential any proprietary, technical or business information disclosed during an engagement, and not to disclose it to third parties without the prior written consent of the disclosing party.

This obligation survives termination for twenty-four (24) months. It does not apply to information that is or becomes public through no fault of the receiving party, was already lawfully known to it, is independently developed without reference to the disclosed information, or is required to be disclosed by law or a competent authority.

The Studio may disclose confidential information to a subcontractor or collaborator engaged on the Client's work, provided that person is bound by confidentiality obligations no less protective than these. The Studio remains responsible for their compliance.

10

Annexe A · Data protection

This annexe applies where the Studio processes personal data on the Client's behalf in the course of an engagement, which includes any user research, usability testing or analytics work. In that processing the Client is the data controller and the Studio is the data processor, as those terms are used in the Data Protection Act, 2019.

For personal data the Studio collects for its own purposes, such as enquiries made through this website, the Studio is the controller and its Privacy Policy applies instead.

Processor obligations

  • Purpose limitation: the Studio processes personal data only on the Client's documented instructions and only as necessary to deliver the engagement.
  • Confidentiality: anyone authorised to process the data is bound by a duty of confidence.
  • Security: the Studio applies technical and organisational measures appropriate to the risk, consistent with Section 41 of the Act.
  • Sub-processors: the Studio engages no sub-processor for Client personal data without the Client's prior written consent, and remains responsible for any it engages.
  • Assistance: the Studio assists the Client in responding to data subject requests and, where required, in consultation with the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner.
  • Breach notification: the Studio notifies the Client without undue delay, and in any event within 48 hours, of becoming aware of a personal data breach affecting Client data.
  • Audit: the Studio makes available the information reasonably necessary to demonstrate compliance with this annexe.
  • Return or deletion: on completion or termination the Studio returns or deletes Client personal data at the Client's election, except where retention is required by law.

Cross-border transfer

Where processing involves transferring personal data outside Kenya, the Studio ensures the safeguards required by Section 48 of the Act are in place, and identifies the destination and the basis for transfer in the Scope of Work.

11

Governing law & dispute resolution

These Terms are governed by and construed in accordance with the laws of the Republic of Kenya. Subject to the dispute resolution procedure below, the courts of Kenya seated in Nairobi have exclusive jurisdiction.

Applicable legislation

  • Law of Contract Act, Cap 23: governs formation, interpretation and enforcement of the engagement.
  • Data Protection Act, 2019: governs the processing of personal data, including Annexe A above.
  • Copyright Act, Cap 130: governs intellectual property in the deliverables.
  • Computer Misuse and Cybercrimes Act, 2018: governs unauthorised access and data interference.
  • Kenya Information and Communications Act (KICA): governs electronic transactions and digital communications.

Note: the Consumer Protection Act, 2012 applies to consumer transactions. An engagement under these Terms is a business-to-business supply and that Act is not intended to apply to it. Retail purchases made through the Studio's shop are consumer transactions and are governed separately by the terms of sale published with them.

Dispute resolution

Before formal proceedings, both parties will attempt to resolve a dispute by good-faith negotiation. If unresolved within thirty (30) days, either party may refer it to mediation under the Nairobi Centre for International Arbitration (NCIA) rules. If mediation does not resolve it within sixty (60) days, either party may pursue remedies in the Kenyan courts.

Carve-out

This procedure does not prevent either party from seeking urgent injunctive or interim relief from a court at any time, nor the Studio from commencing proceedings to recover an undisputed debt.
