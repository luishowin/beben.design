"SEO included" can mean almost anything. At one end it is a set of technical basics that make the site findable. At the other it is an ongoing content and link-building programme with a monthly fee.

Both are legitimate. They are not the same job.

This is what I include at launch, and what I deliberately leave out.

## What foundational SEO covers

The goal is simple: make it possible for a search engine to understand the site, and for a person to see why a result is worth clicking.

At launch, that means:

- **A clear title on every page.** The title is the line in the result. It should say what the page is and who it is for.
- **A useful description.** Not a keyword list. A sentence that earns the click.
- **One clear H1 and a logical heading structure.** People and search engines both use it to understand the page.
- **Descriptive URLs.** `/services/websites/` is easier to read than `/?page_id=42`.
- **A sitemap.** A list of pages for search engines to crawl.
- **Search Console setup.** So you can see what is indexed and what people search for.
- **Internal links.** Pages that point to related pages, with link text that makes sense.
- **Semantic HTML.** Headings, lists, buttons and forms that mean what they say.
- **Speed and mobile basics.** A page that loads and works on a phone.
- **Metadata for sharing.** So a link looks right in WhatsApp, Slack or LinkedIn.

That is the foundation. It does not guarantee a position.

## What it does not get you

Foundational SEO is not:

- **A ranking guarantee.** Nobody can promise a position on Google, because the ranking is not theirs to promise.
- **A traffic number.** Traffic depends on demand, competition and content.
- **A content programme.** Publishing useful pages regularly is a separate, ongoing job.
- **Link building.** Earning links from other sites is its own discipline.
- **Local SEO.** A Google Business Profile, reviews and location pages are a separate scope.
- **Paid search.** Ads are a different budget and a different skill.
- **Conversion work.** A page can rank and still fail to turn a visit into an enquiry.

If a quote bundles all of that into one line, ask what is actually being delivered each month.

## How to check the basics yourself

You do not need a tool subscription to check the foundation:

1. Search `site:yourdomain.com` and see what is indexed.
2. Open a page, view the source, and read the `<title>` and description.
3. Check that each page has one H1.
4. Open the site on a phone and see if the text is readable without zooming.
5. Submit the sitemap in Search Console and check the coverage report.
6. Click three internal links and see if they go where you expect.
7. Paste the URL into a chat app and see what preview appears.

If those are wrong, the expensive SEO programme is starting on a broken surface.

## What to ask a designer

- Which pages get titles and descriptions?
- Is a sitemap generated, and is it submitted?
- Is Search Console set up under my account?
- What happens to the old URLs if the site changes?
- What is included after launch, and what is a separate monthly service?

A good answer is specific and short. A bad answer is a list of acronyms.

## The honest version

Foundational SEO makes the site legible. It gives you a fair chance to be found for the words that describe what you do. It does not create demand, and it does not replace content, links or a business that people already want.

That is why I include it in the [Websites service](/services/#websites) and do not sell a ranking promise. If you need ongoing content or a local search programme, that is a separate conversation with a specialist, and I will say so.

The foundation is worth doing properly at launch. It is not the whole building.
